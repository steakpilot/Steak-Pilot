# SteakPilot business model

## North star

Build a product that home cooks trust enough to use repeatedly, recommend, and eventually pay for. Profitability is the objective; it is not yet proven.

## Initial customer

A beginner or inconsistent home steak cook who owns ordinary cooking equipment, wants a specific step-by-step plan, and finds static recipes or generic timers too uncertain during the cook.

## Core problem

The difficult part is not discovering a steak recipe. It is coordinating heat, flips, basting, checking, recovery, and resting while the cook's hands and attention are occupied. SteakPilot's wedge is a hands-free, adaptive cooking session with visible recovery controls and honest verification cues.

## Value hypotheses

| Hypothesis | How to test | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guided timing reduces anxiety and missed actions | Observe first cooks and collect post-cook feedback | Users complete without coaching and report clearer control than their usual method |
| A successful first cook leads to repeat use | Measure anonymous return cooks over a defined window | A meaningful cohort starts another cook without prompting |
| Personal equipment calibration creates paid value | Prototype the explanation and pricing after repeat use exists | Returning users understand it and show willingness to pay |
| Premium convenience can monetize without weakening safety | Test paid concepts such as history, profiles, multiple steaks, watch, or probe support | Conversion interest without reduced core completion or trust |

No threshold in this table is final until the Phase 2 measurement plan defines cohort size, time window, and success criteria.

## Recommended monetization order

1. Keep the complete core cooking session free during private beta.
2. Establish first-cook completion, credible result feedback, repeat use, and support burden.
3. Interview active repeat users about the value of saved equipment calibration and history.
4. Test one simple paid package or founding-user purchase before building subscriptions.
5. Use subscription pricing only if the product develops recurring value that clearly exceeds occasional timer use.

## Metrics to define before beta instrumentation

- setup started and completed;
- guided cook started and completed;
- checkpoint, Skip, Undo, Queue Flip, Target Reached, and early-end usage;
- notification permission outcome and session restoration;
- optional self-reported result and confidence;
- repeat cook within a defined period;
- crash-free cooking sessions and support contacts per completed cook.

Collect the minimum necessary data, document it accurately, avoid raw cooking-session surveillance, and provide deletion or consent behavior when required.

## Current business risks

- Static recipes and free timers are abundant, so the product must prove materially better execution and repeat value.
- Many users cook steak infrequently, which may limit subscription fit.
- Bad timing guidance can damage an expensive meal and trust in one session.
- Store discovery alone is not an acquisition strategy.
- Supporting iOS and Android too early can double QA and support cost before product-market evidence exists.
